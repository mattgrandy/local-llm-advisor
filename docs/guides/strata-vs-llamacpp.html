Strata vs llama.cpp for local MoE models

Strata helps most when a MoE model is too big for the GPU's VRAM, because it keeps experts in system RAM. On an RTX 3060 12GB, Strata decoded Qwen3.6 35B-A3B at 36 tok/s short-context versus 32 tok/s for llama.cpp with expert offload. When the same model fits fully in VRAM, llama.cpp is faster: 148 tok/s versus Strata's 100 tok/s on an RTX 3090. At ~35K context on the RTX 3060 12GB, llama.cpp stayed ahead (27 vs 12 tok/s). For Qwen3.8 Flash Next, turning MTP drafting on raised short-context decode from 45 to 66 tok/s on the RTX 3060 12GB. We do not treat the RTX 5060 Ti 16GB Strata numbers for Qwen3.6 35B-A3B as a fair head-to-head: that Strata run was on a busy shared host (CPU load average about 90–150). The llama.cpp figures for that card come from a quieter rerun. On this Strata build, MTP drafts never engaged for Qwen3.6 35B-A3B on any card we tested, so we would turn MTP off for that model. Plan for a lot of host RAM: RTX 3060 12GB used about 62 GB while running Qwen3.8 Flash Next IQ3_S.

The short answer

Strata helps most when a MoE model is too big for the GPU's VRAM, because it keeps experts in system RAM. On an RTX 3060 12GB, Strata decoded Qwen3.6 35B-A3B at 36 tok/s short-context versus 32 tok/s for llama.cpp with expert offload. When the same model fits fully in VRAM, llama.cpp is faster: 148 tok/s versus Strata's 100 tok/s on an RTX 3090. At ~35K context on the RTX 3060 12GB, llama.cpp stayed ahead (27 vs 12 tok/s). For Qwen3.8 Flash Next, turning MTP drafting on raised short-context decode from 45 to 66 tok/s on the RTX 3060 12GB. We do not treat the RTX 5060 Ti 16GB Strata numbers for Qwen3.6 35B-A3B as a fair head-to-head: that Strata run was on a busy shared host (CPU load average about 90–150). The llama.cpp figures for that card come from a quieter rerun. On this Strata build, MTP drafts never engaged for Qwen3.6 35B-A3B on any card we tested, so we would turn MTP off for that model. Plan for a lot of host RAM: RTX 3060 12GB used about 62 GB while running Qwen3.8 Flash Next IQ3_S.

Strata is a MoE inference engine, not a llama.cpp backend. These numbers are kept out of the llama.cpp medians used elsewhere on the site. We used Strata 0.2.7 (build 0.1.53, engine 0.1.52) and did not rent extra cards for this comparison.

FAQ

Should I use Strata or llama.cpp?

Strata helps most when a MoE model is too big for the GPU's VRAM, because it keeps experts in system RAM. On an RTX 3060 12GB, Strata decoded Qwen3.6 35B-A3B at 36 tok/s short-context versus 32 tok/s for llama.cpp with expert offload. The llama.cpp figures for that card come from a quieter rerun. On this Strata build, MTP drafts never engaged for Qwen3.6 35B-A3B on any card we tested, so we would turn MTP off for that model. Why are the RTX 5060 Ti Qwen3.6 Strata numbers caveated?

That Strata sweep ran on a busy shared host (CPU load average about 90–150 from other tenants). The load mostly hurts CPU-side expert work. The llama.cpp figures for the same card come from a quieter rerun (load average about 11–51), so they should not be lined up as a tight head-to-head. We do not have a quiet-host Strata run for Qwen3.6 on this card.

Does MTP always help?

No. On this Strata build, MTP drafts never engaged for Qwen3.6 35B-A3B (0 drafts on every card), so turning MTP off is the better setting for that model. On Qwen3.8 Flash-Next, MTP was accepted often and raised decode speed.

How much RAM does Strata need?

It keeps experts in system RAM. Our RTX 3060 12GB run of Qwen3.8 Flash Next IQ3_S used about 62 GB of host RAM.

Where is the raw data?

In the benchmark dataset and on the model pages (Qwen3.6 35B-A3B, Qwen3.8 Flash-Next).
